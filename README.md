# Robust-Linear-Model-Using-Heavy-Tailed-Error
Basically developed for modelling through the robust linear model for which the error term follows, rather than the Gaussian one, either the skewed Student’s t, Student’s t, or symmetric alpha-stable distribution.
It is well known that the linear regression model, represented in simple case as Y= beta0 + beta1*X + epsilon, works based on three underlying assumptions for the innovation (or error) term 'epsilon' including: normality, independence, and variance stability. How well the linear regression model work depends on these three assumptions. If one of these assumptions is dropped, then there is no longer guarantee that the linear regression model works well. Although some approaches such as weighted regression and normality transformation are suggested in practice in order to treat this issue(s), but finding a suitable weight or normality transformation depends on the sample evidence and its size. Moreover, the ordinary least square (OLS) principle no longer guarantees reliable estimators of 'beta0' and 'beta1' in the presence of outlier(s). Herein, we apply two classes of heavier and skew models, that is the skew Student's t and skew slash normal, for the innovation term rather than the usual normal one when one or all of the underlying assumptions is dropped. For more detailed information on latter two classes of distributions, we refer reader to R. M. Basso, V. H. Lachosa, C. R. B. Cabralb, P. Ghosh (2010), Robust mixture modeling based on scale mixtures of skew-normal distributions, Computational Statistics and Data Analysis, 54, 2926-2941, http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/j.csda.2009.09.031.
